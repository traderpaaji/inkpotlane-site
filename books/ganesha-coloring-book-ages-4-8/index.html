A look inside

Who it's for

Children aged 4 to 8 who are meeting Bappa for the first time, or who already shout "Ganpati Bappa Morya!" louder than anyone else in the house. The pictures are big and bold with no fiddly detail, so a young child can finish one in a sitting.

Every page carries one line about the picture and one or two lines of learning: what the big ears, the broken tusk, the mouse and the modak have been teaching children for a very long time. It works as a coloring book and as a gentle first book of the stories. If you would like to tell the story before you open the book, we have written it out for children here.

How to use it

  • Read the story pages in order. The book begins with the story of Ganesh Chaturthi in twelve pictures, from Parvati's clay boy to the farewell at the sea. They read like a picture book.
  • Talk about the lesson line. Each picture has a short lesson underneath. Reading it aloud and asking "what do you think that means?" turns ten minutes of coloring into a conversation.
  • Try the "improve your art" pages last. Three pages at the end ask your child to finish a half-drawn Bappa, copy a murti square by square and design their own Bappa.
  • Hang the finished pages up. Every picture is printed on one side only, so a finished page can go up for the festival without losing the next one.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between the 4-8 and the 8-12 editions?

Both begin with the same story in twelve pictures. The 4-8 edition then has big, bold pictures of Bappa in his many looks, with simple lessons. The 8-12 edition has detailed drawings of 32 murti styles from across India, for children who have outgrown thick-line coloring.

Does it explain why Ganesha has an elephant head?

Yes. The story pages tell how Parvati made a boy from clay, how he guarded her door, and how Shiva gave him the head of a wise elephant, with the lesson that the big ears are for listening and the small eyes for focus.

How big are the pages?

8.5 x 11 inches, 94 pages in all, with 45 pictures, printed on white paper with a glossy cover.

Is it only for Ganesh Chaturthi?

No. It makes a good gift for a birthday, a new school year or any day that needs a good beginning, which is exactly what Ganesha stands for.
