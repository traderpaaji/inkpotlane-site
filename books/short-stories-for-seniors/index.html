Who it's for

For the quiet half hour after supper, when the evening news has been too much. This Kindle eBook collects thirty true stories about strangers who were kind to each other: not saints, and for the most part not famous people. It suits anyone who would rather end the day with something kind, and it makes a thoughtful gift for a mother, grandmother or friend with a new Kindle.

Inside the book

  • Thirty true stories, each one a short single sitting, from London in the 1730s to Berlin in 1989, arranged so that no two in a row are much alike.
  • Kindness between strangers in every one. A young woman on a mule carries library books up a Kentucky creek. A lighthouse keeper's daughter rows into a storm with her father. A busy newspaper editor answers a little girl's letter about Santa Claus. A banker sets up a plank across two barrels on a burned-out waterfront and lends to anyone who wants to rebuild. A man who was once handed twenty dollars in a diner spends the rest of his life handing it on.
  • "Where this comes from" under every story: at least two separate sources named, such as museums, national archives, universities and long-established newspapers, and a plain word wherever the record is uncertain or a favorite legend turns out not to be true.
  • A full list of sources at the back, with web addresses, for anyone who wants to read more.
  • A short welcome and a closing page that speak to the reader, not just the history.
  • About 37,000 words, plus the list of sources.

How to use it

  • Read one an evening, or dip in anywhere. Each story stands on its own. Read in order, they travel from a Kentucky creek to the North Atlantic to an Iowa farm in a few evenings.
  • Set the words to the size that suits you. On any Kindle, tablet or phone, the text can be made as large or as small as is comfortable.
  • Look at the note. If a story surprises you, the note under it says where it comes from and where the sources disagree.

One of the thirty is the story of a relief train that left Boston thirteen hours after the Halifax Explosion of 1917. Why does Nova Scotia send a Christmas tree to Boston? tells how that train led to a thank-you that is still sent every December.

Frequently asked questions

Are the stories really true?

Yes. Every one really happened, and each has a note naming at least two sources. Where the record is uncertain, the story says so, and where a favorite detail turns out not to be true, it says what did happen instead.

Are any of the stories sad?

Some take place in wartime or after a flood or a storm, because hard times are when kindness shows most clearly. Each is told gently, and every one ends with someone who showed up.

How long is each story?

Each one is a short single sitting, a few quiet minutes. The whole book is about 37,000 words, plus the list of sources.

Can I make the text bigger?

Yes. It is a Kindle eBook, so you can set the text size on any Kindle, or in the Kindle app on a phone or tablet. It is also in Kindle Unlimited.
