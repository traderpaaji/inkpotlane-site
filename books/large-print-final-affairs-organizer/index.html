A look inside

Who it's for

Anyone who would rather their family never spend a long, anxious year hunting for what was never written down. It is for the parent or grandparent who sits with a cup of tea and a good pen and fills in a few pages at a time, in their own hand. And it is for the son or daughter who gives it with the kindest sentence there is: "fill it in when you're ready, no hurry."

The print is large on purpose — every line 16 point or larger, labels included — because the hand filling this in and the eyes that will one day read it back both deserve to see it clearly. It is not a sad book to sit with. Most people find it the same quiet relief as making a will: heavy for a minute, then lighter than they have felt in a while. Every page you finish is one less thing your family will have to guess at.

Inside the book

  • A letter before you begin, and a plain page that says what this is — an organizer, never a will, and never legal, financial or medical advice.
  • The one rule that keeps it safe. It records where things are kept — never a password, a PIN or a full account number. So it is far safer to keep and to hand on, because it reduces the risk of exposing sensitive credentials even if the wrong person ever opens it. The rule is printed again on every sensitive page.
  • 14 sections, one life-area each: about me, people to call first, important documents and where they are, financial accounts, insurance, home and vehicles, monthly bills, digital life, medical information and wishes, pets, funeral and memorial preferences, things to pass on, letters to loved ones, and a page to keep it all current.
  • Generous room to write, in spacious large-print forms — one thing per page, so you can fill in a page in ten minutes and stop without losing your place.
  • Space for letters in your own words, for the things that are not on any form.

How to use it

  • Work at your own pace, page by page. Fill in what applies, skip what does not, and come back whenever something changes. There is no pressure to finish everything at once.
  • Never write a password, PIN or full account number. Only ever record where those things are kept. This is what makes the book safe to leave in a drawer.
  • Keep it somewhere safe when you're done, and tell one or two people you trust where it is. A book like this only helps if the right person can find it.
  • Review it once a year. Lives change — a new account, a moved document, a different wish. The last section is a place to keep it current.

Frequently asked questions

Is this a will, or a legal document?

No. It is an organizer, not a will, and not legal, financial or medical advice. It records where your important things are kept and the preferences you would like known. The legal part — the will itself — is for you and a professional; this book simply points to it.

Is it upsetting to fill in?

For most people, no. It tends to feel the way making a will does: a little heavy for a moment, then a real relief. The tone throughout is warm, dignified and plain-spoken — never morbid, never clinical. It is written as a kindness to your family, and that is how it reads.

Is it safe to keep in the house?

Yes. The book never holds a password, PIN or full account number — only a note of where those things are kept. That is a deliberate feature: it points trusted people toward what matters without ever becoming a book of access credentials.

How large is the print?

Large throughout — every line is 16 point or larger, labels included. It is made for a reader whose eyes tire, and for the family member who will one day read it back.

How big is it?

8.5 x 11 inches, 138 pages, printed in black ink on white paper.
