A look inside

Who it's for

Children aged 4 to 8, and the grown-up reading beside them. The lines are thick and the shapes are big, so a small hand can finish a whole picture without giving up halfway, and nothing is so fiddly that it ends in tears on Christmas Eve.

It is made to be read aloud. Each of the first 27 pictures has one couplet of Clement Clarke Moore's poem printed underneath, so you read a line, your child looks for the mouse, and then the coloring starts. If you would like to read the poem first, the full text and its story are here.

How to use it

  • Read one page a night. Start on 28 November and the 27th couplet lands on Christmas Eve, so the book becomes a countdown as well as a coloring book.
  • Find the mouse first. A small mouse in a Santa hat hides in every one of the 50 pictures. Looking for him gets children to study the whole page before they pick up a crayon, and it keeps older brothers and sisters interested too.
  • Tear out the finished ones. Every picture is on its own sheet with nothing printed on the back, so a finished page can go on the fridge without losing the next one.
  • Use what you have. Crayons, colored pencils and water-based markers all work well. Very wet markers can still soak through paper, so slip a piece of card behind the page. There is more on that in our guide to markers that don't bleed through.

Frequently asked questions

Is the whole poem in the book?

Almost. The book follows the 1823 poem couplet by couplet, with one change: the couplet about St. Nick's pipe is left out, because the book is made for young children. Everything else is there, in order.

What age is it for?

Ages 4 to 8. Younger children enjoy it with a grown-up helping; older ones like the hunt for the mouse and coloring the detail in the bonus scenes.

How big are the pages?

8.5 x 11 inches, a standard letter-size paperback, 104 pages in all, with 50 pictures.

Will markers show through to the next picture?

No. Each picture is printed on one side of the sheet only, so even if a marker soaks through, it lands on a blank page, not on the next picture.
