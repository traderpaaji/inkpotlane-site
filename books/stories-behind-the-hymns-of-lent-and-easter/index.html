Who it's for

Perhaps it happens on the first Sunday of Lent. The organ begins "Forty Days and Forty Nights," and somewhere in the second verse you realize you have sung these words for years without once asking who wrote them, or why. This Kindle eBook is for that moment.

It is for choir members and everyone who sings from the pews; for choir directors, organists and pastors; and for readers who like to keep Lent with one quiet reading a day. You do not need to read music or know any church history.

Inside the book

  • 41 readings: forty for the traditional forty days of Lent, from Ash Wednesday to Holy Saturday with the Sundays left free, and one for Easter morning. The readings are numbered, not dated, so the book can be used in any year.
  • A journey through the season, from "Saviour, When in Dust to Thee" at the start of Lent, through the hymns of Palm Sunday and Holy Week, to "Christ the Lord Is Risen To-day" at Easter.
  • The people behind the words: bishops and scholars, a young schoolteacher in New York, a Quaker poet, a Scottish minister who was nearly blind, a former master of slave ships who wrote "Amazing Grace," and the unnamed singers of an African American spiritual.
  • History told apart from legend. Where a favorite story cannot be proved, the chapter says so and explains what the record does show.
  • Many traditions: hymns and stories from Anglican, Catholic, Methodist, Baptist, Lutheran, Presbyterian, Quaker and Moravian backgrounds, along with shape-note singing and the African American spiritual, each told with respect.
  • The same simple shape every day: some of the hymn's words, its story in short sections, a brief reflection, and a "Where this comes from" note naming the sources.

How to use it

  • Begin on Ash Wednesday. Read one chapter a day except Sundays, and you reach Reading 40 on Holy Saturday and Reading 41 on Easter Day, whatever the date of Easter. In 2027, Ash Wednesday falls on 10 February and Easter Day on 28 March.
  • Or turn straight to this Sunday's hymn. Each chapter stands on its own.
  • Bring a hymnal if you have one. This is a devotional history to read, not a hymnal: it prints selected hymn words only, with no music.

A note on the words

The hymn words come from printings of 1930 or earlier, so some lines may differ from those in hymnals today; where a later editor changed a familiar line, the chapter says so. Scripture is quoted from the World English Bible (Updated edition), a modern translation.

For the other end of the church year, Stories Behind the Christmas Carols tells the true history of twenty-five carols, one chapter a day from 1 December to Christmas Day.

Frequently asked questions

Is this a hymnal?

No. It is a devotional history to read. It prints selected hymn words only, with no music or musical notation; tunes and composers are named in the stories.

Do I have to start on Ash Wednesday?

No. The readings are numbered, not dated, and each chapter stands on its own, so you can begin any day or turn to the hymn your choir is singing this week.

Which hymns are in it?

Forty-one hymns of Lent, Holy Week and Easter, among them "Forty Days and Forty Nights," "Amazing Grace," "It Is Well with My Soul," "Rock of Ages," "Were You There" and "Christ the Lord Is Risen To-day."

How long is it?

About 50,000 words: forty-one chapters, each one a quiet reading for the start or end of a day. It is a Kindle eBook, and it is in Kindle Unlimited.
