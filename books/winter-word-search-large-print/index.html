Winter Word Search Large Print for Seniors and Adults

A Cozy Puzzle Book of 90 Easy-to-Read Grids Whose Hidden Messages Follow One Winter, from First Frost to First Snowdrop

Ninety big, clear word searches for slow, snowy afternoons, whose hidden lines follow one winter from the first frost to the first snowdrop.

Who it's for

Adults and seniors who like big letters and a calm, cozy hour on a slow, snowy afternoon. Every puzzle hides a short line from one winter seen from a warm chair: the first frost on the glass, small birds at the feeder, soup on the stove, the longest night, the slow return of the light and, at last, the first snowdrop by the gate.

It is a winter book, not a Christmas book, so it lasts the whole season: ninety puzzles is about one for each day of December, January and February.

Inside the book

  • How to Play, with a page map of the levels and a small worked example of the hidden message.
  • 90 puzzles in three levels. Puzzles 1 to 30 are easy, with words running across, down and diagonally down to the right. Puzzles 31 to 60 are medium and add words that read backward and diagonally upward. Puzzles 61 to 90 are harder and use all eight directions.
  • A winter note on every puzzle page: a gentle prompt or a small, true winter fact, such as why a snowy morning sounds so hushed.
  • Themed word lists: snow and frost, birds at the feeder, warm drinks, soups and baking, stars, skating, maple sugaring and more.
  • A hidden message in every grid. Read in order, the ninety messages become one quiet winter diary, "The Winter Window," printed in full at the back.
  • All 90 answers at the back, in large print, each with its hidden message.

How to use it

  • Start at Puzzle 1. The levels are gentle steps, and How to Play shows what changes at each one.
  • Read the leftovers. When every word is circled, read the unused letters from left to right and top to bottom. They spell that page's line of the winter diary.
  • Use a pencil and a good lamp. As one of the winter notes says, big letters love good light.

Curious about the science in the snow? Why do snowflakes have six sides? tells the story of the Vermont farmer who first photographed a snow crystal, and the answer he helped uncover. For the months after winter, there is a companion Spring Word Search.

Frequently asked questions

How big is the print?

Grid letters are 20 to 24 point, word lists are 16 point, and the answers are 16 to 18 point.

How hard are the puzzles?

They get harder in three steps of thirty: words that read forward, then backward and upward words too, then all eight directions.

Is it a Christmas book?

No. It follows the whole winter, from the first frost to the first snowdrop, so it is just as good in January and February as in December.

What is the hidden message?

When every word is found, the letters left over spell a short line. Read in order, the ninety lines make one winter diary, printed in full at the back.

How big is the book?

8.5 x 11 inches, 122 pages, printed in black ink on white paper.
