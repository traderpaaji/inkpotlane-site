A look inside

Who it's for

Children aged 8 to 12 who want something more than thick outlines: detailed drawings that take an afternoon, and a reason to look closely. Each picture is a different murti style from somewhere in India, and each one is named, so the book quietly becomes a tour of how Bappa is made and dressed from Maharashtra to Tamil Nadu, Gujarat and Odisha.

Every page carries the name of the murti style, one line about what makes it special, and one or two lines of learning drawn from the stories of Ganesha. If you are curious about the styles themselves, we have written a short guide to them here.

How to use it

  • Start with the story. The book opens with the story of Ganesh Chaturthi in twelve pictures, the same story as the younger edition, drawn for an older hand.
  • Use fine tools for the detail. Colored pencils and fine-tip markers suit the jewellery, crowns and patterns. Very wet markers can soak through paper, so slip a sheet of card behind the page.
  • Read the lesson, then look again. The lesson lines point at something in the picture: the raised hand that says "do not fear", the big ears that say "listen more, speak less".
  • Try the two art pages at the end. One asks your child to copy a murti square by square; the other shows how to draw Ganesha step by step.

Frequently asked questions

How is it different from the 4-8 edition?

The story pages are the same. After that, the 4-8 edition has big, bold pictures of Bappa in simple settings; this edition has 48 detailed designs, including 32 named murti styles from across India.

Which murti styles are in it?

The classic Pen looks with pagdi, singhasan and mukut, the grand pandal looks of Mumbai, Pune and Hyderabad, Bappa with Shiva's family, as Ram and as Krishna, resting on Sheshnag, several of the old forms such as Vakratunda and Heramba, three balancing murtis, and the clay, mirror-work, filigree and temple-stone traditions of Gujarat, Odisha and Tamil Nadu.

Will markers show through?

Each picture is printed on one side only, so even if a marker soaks through, it lands on a blank page and not on the next picture.

How big are the pages?

8.5 x 11 inches, 100 pages in all, printed on white paper with a glossy cover.
