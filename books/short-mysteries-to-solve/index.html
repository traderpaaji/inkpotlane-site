Who it's for

For readers who like a mystery they can solve themselves, this Kindle eBook collects twelve cozy whodunit short stories set in Gullcombe, an invented harbor town. They run from New Year's Day into spring, and each one follows Maud Tremayne, the town's piano tuner, whose trade is listening and who hears everything.

Read one story at a sitting, or several in an evening. Each story stops before its answer so you can name the culprit first, and the solutions are kept together at the back of the book, away from the stories.

It is a Kindle eBook, so it reads on a Kindle, a phone or a tablet, and it is in Kindle Unlimited.

Inside the book

  • Twelve small mysteries, in the order of the calendar: The Switched Boots, The Baker's Bicycle, The Quiet Collection, The Two Clocks, The Gramophone, The Causeway at Noon, The Misprinted Programme, The Salted Spring, The Bell That Did Not Ring, The Honest Thief, The Festival Cup and The Tune in the Piano.
  • Everyday puzzles. A new pair of sea-boots walks off in the wrong feet. A bread bicycle vanishes in two minutes. Two town clocks that never agree hide a theft between them. A silver oar disappears behind a bell that should have rung and did not.
  • Every clue on the page. Each story stops at a "Pause and solve" box. Everything you need to answer its question has already appeared in the story.
  • Solutions that show the reasoning. All twelve solutions are at the back. Each one walks through the reasoning clue by clue, and in this eBook each clue links straight back to the place in the story where it appeared.
  • A thirteenth mystery. All spring, in every piano Maud opens, she finds a few bars of a handwritten tune. Who is leaving them, and why? That question runs through all twelve stories.
  • Cozy and kind. No gore, no violence, and nobody dies: just a warm little town, a sharp old sleuth and the pleasure of naming the culprit before she does.

How to use it

  • Read in order if you can. The stories follow the calendar rather than a difficulty scale, and the thirteenth mystery builds from story to story.
  • Stop at the box. When you reach "Pause and solve", think it through and look back over the story if you like before you go to the solution.
  • Follow the links. If a clue in a solution surprises you, tap it and the book takes you back to where it appeared.

New to solve-along stories? What is a whodunit? explains the shape these stories follow, and Short mysteries to solve has an original one to try for free.

The Gullcombe Mysteries

Short Mysteries to Solve is Book 2 of the Gullcombe Mysteries. You do not need to have read the first, Christmas Mysteries to Solve, though you will know the town if you have. That first collection is set in December, from the thirteenth to Christmas Eve; this one begins on New Year's Day and runs into spring.

Frequently asked questions

Can I really solve them before the answer?

Yes. Every clue is on the page before the "Pause and solve" box, and each solution walks through the reasoning clue by clue, so you can check exactly where you were right or where a clue slipped past you.

Do I need to read Book 1 first?

No. Each collection stands on its own. Readers who have met Gullcombe in Christmas Mysteries to Solve will recognize the town.

Is there anything violent in it?

No. There is no gore and no violence, and nobody dies. The mysteries are about switched boots, a missing bicycle, a theft between two clocks, a missing silver oar and other small puzzles of town life.

Is it a paperback?

Not at the moment. It is a Kindle eBook, read on a Kindle device or in the Kindle app on a phone or tablet, and it is in Kindle Unlimited.

Are the stories marked by difficulty?

No. They run in the order of the calendar, not of difficulty, so start at the beginning and let the thirteenth mystery build as you go.
