Who it's for

Adults and seniors who grew up in the 1950s and 1960s, and anyone who loves to hear about those years. Every puzzle is a corner of everyday life back then: wash day, the milkman at dawn, the party line, the corner store, Saturday matinees, the drive-in, the sock hop, tail fins and chrome. The last fifteen puzzles are an old-fashioned Christmas, from the toy catalog to Christmas morning.

It makes a gentle gift for a parent or grandparent, and a quiet, unplugged hour for anyone who loves them.

Inside the book

  • A welcome page that says who the book is for, what level it is and how to use it, then How to Play with a small worked example of the hidden message.
  • 90 puzzles in three levels. Puzzles 1 to 30 are easy, with words running across, down and diagonally down to the right. Puzzles 31 to 60 are medium and add words that read backward and diagonally upward. Puzzles 61 to 90 are a little harder and use all eight directions.
  • A REMEMBER line on every puzzle, to read aloud and share before you start: "REMEMBER the clink of glass bottles on the step before sunrise?"
  • A hidden message in every grid. When every word is found, the leftover letters spell one line. Read in order, the ninety lines tell one gentle story of a year on an old street, gathered in full at the back.
  • A Christmas Past finale, puzzles 76 to 90: the toy catalog, choosing the tree, tinsel and bubble lights, carolers, stockings and Christmas morning.
  • All 90 answers at the back, in large print too.
  • Everyday words only: no brands and no celebrities.

How to use it

  • Read the REMEMBER line first. If someone is with you, read it aloud and swap stories before you start: the best part is often the story that follows.
  • Go in order or dip in anywhere. The levels climb gently, and How to Play shows exactly what changes at each one.
  • Read the leftovers. When every word is circled, read the unused letters from left to right and top to bottom, and write the hidden message on the lines under the grid.

Frequently asked questions

How big is the print?

Grid letters are 20 to 24 point, and everything else is 16 point or larger, the answers included.

How hard are the puzzles?

Gentle. They get harder in three steps: words that read forward, then backward and upward words too, then all eight directions.

Is it all Christmas?

No. Puzzles 1 to 75 are everyday life through the year, and the last fifteen, 76 to 90, are an old-fashioned Christmas.

What if my memories are different?

The words describe everyday life as many people remember it, and your own memories may be different. As the welcome page says, that is half the fun.

How big is the book?

8.5 x 11 inches, 122 pages, printed in black ink on white paper.
