A look inside

Who it's for

Large print word fill-ins are for anyone who likes the look of a crossword but would rather skip the clues, and for anyone choosing a puzzle book for them. Each puzzle gives you an empty criss-cross grid and the full word list. Your job is to place every word across or down so the letters match wherever they meet. You work from lengths and crossings, not trivia questions, and the pleasure is in watching an empty grid fill in.

The print is large all the way through, the book opens with a plain how-to page and a worked example, and every answer is at the back.

Inside the book

  • How to solve a fill-in, first. One page of five simple tips, then a worked example: a tiny three-word grid shown empty and solved, with each step explained.
  • 112 puzzles in 16 cozy themes, 7 to a theme: In the Garden, The Kitchen, Tea Time, At the Seaside, The Cozy Home, Garden Birds, Weather and Seasons, In the Countryside, Music and Song, Books and Reading, Sewing and Crafts, Flowers, Trees and Woodland, Breakfast, The Village and A Country Fair.
  • One puzzle per page, with a roomy grid and the word list underneath, grouped by length (3 letters, 4 letters and so on) in 16 point type.
  • A level on every page. Every theme runs from easy to hard: two Easy puzzles, two Gentle, two Steady and one Tricky. That makes 32 Easy, 32 Gentle, 32 Steady and 16 Tricky across the book.
  • A few starter letters. Seventeen puzzles have one to three letters already printed in the grid to help you begin. The other 95 start with an empty grid.
  • Every grid has one answer. Each puzzle was filled and then re-solved by computer to confirm it has exactly one solution.
  • The answers at the back, every grid shown filled in, with 16 point letters.

How to use it

  • Read the how-to page and the worked example first, even if you do crosswords. A fill-in is solved differently.
  • Start with a word length that appears only once or twice in the list. A word whose length is unique has only one slot it can go in.
  • Use the crossings. Where an across slot and a down slot share a square, both words must have the same letter there.
  • Use a pencil and cross words off the list as you place them, so you always see what is left.

Frequently asked questions

What is a word fill-in?

A crossword grid with no clues. You are given every answer as a list, grouped by length, and your job is to work out where each word goes so that all the crossings agree.

How large is the print?

The word lists, the instructions and the page headings are all 16 point or larger, and the letters in the completed answer grids at the back are 16 point too.

Is it good for beginners?

Yes. The book opens with a how-to page and a worked example, and Easy puzzles begin every theme.

Do I need to know a lot of words?

No. Every word you need is printed on the puzzle page, grouped by length, so there are no clues to work out as there would be in a crossword. The words come from each puzzle's theme, such as In the Garden, The Kitchen or The Village.

Do all the puzzles have starter letters?

No. Seventeen of the 112 puzzles have one to three letters already filled in; the other 95 begin with an empty grid. The five solving tips and the worked example at the front show you how to begin with word lengths and crossings.

Are the answers included?

Yes. Every grid is shown filled in at the back of the book.

How big is the book?

8.5 x 11 inches, 146 pages, printed in black ink on white paper.
