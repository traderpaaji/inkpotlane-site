Who it's for

Anyone who likes a December ritual that fits in a quiet ten minutes, or is buying for someone who does. From 1 December to Christmas Eve this book gives one day of Christmas cryptograms at a time, six puzzles a day, and then six more on Christmas Day. Each puzzle is a line of coded text: crack the letter-swap and a carol line, a quotation from Dickens or from "A Visit from St. Nicholas," a Christmas custom from around the world or a piece of Christmas history appears.

It suits grandparents, parents and anyone who enjoys a puzzle with a point of arrival. It also suits someone who has never solved a cryptogram, because the first days give three letters in every puzzle and a worked example comes first.

Large print Christmas cryptograms: what's inside

  • 25 days, 150 puzzles. Six cryptograms a day from 1 December to Christmas Eve, and six more at mixed levels on Christmas Day. A 25-box countdown page lets you mark off each day.
  • Letters given at first, then fewer. The early days give three letters, then two, then one, and finally none as Christmas approaches, so the solving climbs as the countdown does.
  • Large print throughout. Code letters are 19 point, with every other line of text 16 point or larger, and a numbered key strip sits with every puzzle.
  • One-Letter Help from 13 December. If a puzzle will not budge, one more letter is available without spoiling the rest.
  • A worked example first. A clear guide for first-time cryptogram solvers shows a coded line going from blank to solved.
  • Every answer at the back, with credits for the quoted lines and 60 source notes on the customs and history.

How to use it

  • Treat it like an advent calendar. One day's six puzzles, then stop; the rule is the pleasure.
  • Start on 1 December, or any day you like. The days are numbered, not dated by weekday or year, so the book can be opened in any year.
  • Begin with the short words. A one-letter word is usually A or I. THE and AND are the commonest short words.
  • Use One-Letter Help sparingly. It gives a letter, not the answer.

Curious where the countdown calendar came from? Advent calendar history traces it from chalk lines on a wall to the first printed calendars. New to cryptograms? How to solve cryptograms walks through the method.

Frequently asked questions

How big is the print?

Code letters are 19 point, and all other text is 16 point or larger.

I have never solved a cryptogram. Can I start here?

Yes. A worked example and a clear guide come first, and the first days give three letters in every puzzle.

What will I be decoding?

Lines from traditional carols, from Dickens and from "A Visit from St. Nicholas," Christmas customs from around the world, true pieces of Christmas history and short festive thoughts, with credits for the quoted lines at the back.

Is it dated for a particular year?

No. It runs from 1 December to Christmas Day, with no year and no weekdays printed, so it can be used again next December.

Are the answers included?

Yes, for every puzzle, at the back of the book.

How big is the book?

8.5 x 11 inches, 132 pages, in black ink. A hardcover edition is also available.
