Christmas Gifts for Puzzle Lovers: Match the Puzzle to the Person

Christmas gifts for puzzle lovers, sorted by what they do with a pencil: hunt words, break codes, work out logic or follow a mystery. Five questions first.

Inkpot Lane · 12 November 2026 · 6 min read

On Sunday, 21 December 1913, four days before Christmas, readers of the New York World found something new: a grid of blank squares with a list of clues, which its maker, a journalist named Arthur Wynne, called a "word-cross." It is the puzzle usually cited as the first crossword. The name was later reversed to "cross-word." A hundred and thirteen years on, a puzzle is still one of the few presents a person can open on Christmas morning and start before the coffee is cold. The trick with Christmas gifts for puzzle lovers, as with any gift, is choosing the right one.

The short answer: Buy for how the person solves, not for the word "puzzle." Ask what they do with a pencil. If they hunt, give a word search. If they like to crack a code, give cryptograms. If they like to work things out by elimination, give logic grids. If they like to guess the culprit, give a mystery with every clue on the page. Then check three things: the print size, the starting level, and whether it can be started tonight with nothing but a pencil.

Ask five questions first

You do not need to know the person's whole life. You need to know five small things.

1. When they have ten minutes, do they look for things or work things out? Looking is a word search. Working out is a code, a logic grid or a mystery.

2. Do they like a story around the puzzle? A plain grid suits a person who just wants to switch off. A person who likes a reason for the puzzle will like one where the answers add up to something.

3. Can they see small print comfortably? If there is any doubt, choose large print, and check that the print is large on every page, including the answers. A book labeled "large print" on the cover and set small inside is a mistake you will only find out about the day after Christmas.

4. Are they a beginner or a veteran? Choose a little easy rather than a little hard. A solved puzzle is a reason to open the book tomorrow; a stuck one is a reason to close it.

5. Will they solve alone or at the table with others? Some of these are made for passing around. Others are best done in a chair by the fire.

The word-finder

The word search is a younger puzzle than the crossword. Wikipedia credits it to Norman E. Gibat of Norman, Oklahoma, in the Selenby Digest of 1 March 1968, though it notes that the origin is disputed, with other claimants for earlier versions. Its charm is that it asks for patience and a sharp eye but very little vocabulary, which makes it a friendly gift for a beginner and, when the print is large, for someone whose eyes tire more quickly than their mind.

The code-breaker

Cryptograms were in the newspapers before the crossword was. In December 1839 Edgar Allan Poe invited the readers of Alexander's Weekly Messenger to send him ciphers, and he wrote that he had received about a hundred and solved all but one, which he showed was a string of random characters. A later count of the ciphers in the magazine found 36, of which Poe printed solutions to 24. A cryptogram is usually a quotation with its letters swapped, and its reward is different from a grid's: the solver finishes with a sentence worth reading.

The logic-lover and the mystery-lover

The person who likes to eliminate possibilities will usually like a logic grid, and the one who likes to point at the guilty party will usually like a mystery. Both are puzzles with a story, and a snowbound week in December is the best setting either of them gets. If you are buying for both in one household, you can give each a different kind.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good Christmas gift for someone who loves puzzles?

A book that matches how they solve. Word searches suit people who like to hunt, cryptograms suit code-breakers, logic grids suit people who like to eliminate, and short mysteries suit people who like to guess the culprit.

What should I check before buying a puzzle book as a gift?

Check the print size on every page, the starting level, and whether the answers are included. Large print should mean large print throughout, answers included.

Are puzzle books a good gift for grandparents?

Many people enjoy them, especially in large print, and they need no setup. Choose a level slightly below what you think the person can do, so the first evening is a success.

Can I give a puzzle book as an eBook?

Some of these are Kindle eBooks, such as Christmas Mysteries to Solve, which can be read on a phone, tablet or Kindle. Grid and cryptogram books are better on paper, where the solver can write.
