National Dog Show Groups: The Seven, Explained for Thanksgiving

National Dog Show groups made simple: what the seven groups are, how one dog reaches Best in Show, a quick quiz and a sofa game for Thanksgiving Day.

Inkpot Lane · 12 November 2026 · 10 min read

Every Thanksgiving, somewhere between the last parade float and the first slice of turkey, a family that has never been to a dog show finds itself arguing about terriers. If you have ever wondered what the national dog show groups actually are, and why a Pug can meet a Great Dane only in the final ring, this is the short guide to read before the show starts. It begins with a gap in a television schedule. In 2002 the film NBC had been showing after the parade, It's a Wonderful Life, was not holding viewers. Inspired by the comedy film Best in Show, the network put a real dog show in the slot instead: the Kennel Club of Philadelphia's show, part of a line of Philadelphia dog shows that goes back to 1879. It has been on after the parade every Thanksgiving since.

The short answer: The National Dog Show uses the seven groups of the American Kennel Club: Sporting, Hound, Working, Terrier, Toy, Non-Sporting and Herding. Each breed is judged on its own first. The best dog of each breed then competes inside its group, and the seven group winners meet at the end for Best in Show.

The seven National Dog Show groups at a glance

The American Kennel Club says each group is organized by the original work its breeds were developed to do. That one idea makes the whole afternoon easier to follow. The examples in the last column are breeds the AKC itself lists for each group.

GroupWhat the breeds were developed forBreeds you may know
SportingHelping hunters find and bring back game birdsLabrador Retriever, Cocker Spaniel, German Shorthaired Pointer
HoundHunting, by chasing animals by sight or following their scentGreyhound, Bloodhound, Dachshund
WorkingJobs beside people: pulling sleds and carts, guarding flocks and homesBoxer, Great Dane, Rottweiler
TerrierGoing after rodents and other verminScottish Terrier, West Highland White Terrier, Bull Terrier
ToyBeing small, close companionsChihuahua, Pug, Shih Tzu
Non-SportingA mixed group of breeds whose jobs do not fit the other sixBulldog, Dalmatian, Poodle (Standard and Miniature)
HerdingMoving livestock such as sheep and cattleBorder Collie, German Shepherd Dog, Pembroke Welsh Corgi

The Non-Sporting group is the one that puzzles people, because its name says only what the dogs are not. Think of it as the drawer for everything that does not belong in the other six, and it stops being a mystery. Poodles are split by size: the Standard and the Miniature are shown in Non-Sporting, and the Toy Poodle in the Toy group.

How one dog gets to Best in Show

A dog show is a ladder with three rungs.

  1. Breed. Every dog first competes only against dogs of its own breed. One is chosen as the best of that breed.
  2. Group. The breed winners go into the ring with the other breed winners from their group. One dog wins each group.
  3. Best in Show. The seven group winners meet in the final ring, and one dog is chosen.

Here is the part that surprises first-time viewers. In the group ring the judge is not deciding whether a Chihuahua is "better" than a Pug. In the AKC's own words, "Dogs are not being compared to each other." Each dog is measured against the written standard for its own breed, the description of what an ideal example of that breed looks and moves like. The judge is asking which dog in the ring comes closest to its own standard. That is how a small dog can beat a very large one, and why the final seven can look as if they have nothing in common.

It also explains a rule that disappoints many viewers: mixed-breed dogs cannot enter. The AKC's explanation is that these shows exist to evaluate breeding stock: the closer a dog is to its breed's standard, the better its chance of producing puppies that meet it. For the same reason, purebred dogs that have been spayed or neutered cannot enter either.

How a Philadelphia dog show ended up on Thanksgiving

The show you watch is older than television. Purebred dogs were first shown in Philadelphia at the Centennial Exposition of 1876, and the Kennel Club of Philadelphia and the clubs that came before it have been holding dog shows since 1879. The shows ran until 1927, stopped from 1928 to 1932, and started again in 1933. They have been held every year since.

NBC had lost its Thanksgiving football four years before, and in 2002 it began televising the Philadelphia show on Thanksgiving Day under the name most people now know, The National Dog Show. The dogs are judged before the holiday, not on it. In 2026 the show is held on November 14 and 15 at the Greater Philadelphia Expo Center in Oaks, Pennsylvania, and the Saturday show is the one taped for television, so what you see on Thanksgiving is recorded, not live. NBC shows it on Thanksgiving Day, straight after the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, and the show's own website gives the time as noon to 2 p.m. local time. In 2026, Thanksgiving is Thursday, November 26. The hosts the website names are the actor John O'Hurley, David Frei and Mary Carillo. Check your local NBC listing in case your station is different.

The recent winners show how open the final is. In 2023 the winner was a Sealyham Terrier called Stache, from the Terrier group. In 2024 it was Vito, a Pug, from the Toy group. In 2025 it was Soleil, a Belgian Sheepdog, from the Herding group. Three years, three different groups. Before them a Scottish Deerhound named Claire, from the Hound group, won in both 2020 and 2021, the first dog in the show's history to win two years running.

A sofa game: pick your seven

David Frei, one of the show's hosts, has a name for rooting for your own breed: the "alma mater factor," the same pull that makes people cheer for their old school. You can turn that into a game that needs only paper and a pencil.

  1. Before each group is judged, everyone writes down the breed they think will win it. No changing your mind once the judge starts walking the line.
  2. Score one point for each group winner you pick.
  3. Before the final, everyone picks one of the seven group winners. A correct Best in Show pick scores three points.
  4. Whoever has the most points chooses the first slice of pie.

Children can play on nearly equal terms with adults: unless there is a dog show regular in the family, everyone is guessing.

Frequently asked questions

What are the 7 groups in the National Dog Show?

Sporting, Hound, Working, Terrier, Toy, Non-Sporting and Herding. They are the seven groups the American Kennel Club uses at its shows, and each is organized by the work its breeds were first developed to do.

How does a dog win Best in Show?

It has to win three times: first against the other dogs of its own breed, then against the other breed winners in its group, and finally against the six other group winners. At every stage each dog is measured against the standard for its own breed.

What time is the National Dog Show on Thanksgiving?

NBC shows it on Thanksgiving Day, straight after the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, and the show's own website gives the time as noon to 2 p.m. local time. In 2026 that is Thursday, November 26. Check your local listing in case your station is different.

Can mixed-breed dogs compete in the National Dog Show?

No. The American Kennel Club says these shows exist to evaluate breeding stock against each breed's written standard, so mixed-breed dogs are not eligible, and neither are purebred dogs that have been spayed or neutered.

Is the National Dog Show the same as Westminster?

No. They are separate shows. The National Dog Show is held by the Kennel Club of Philadelphia in November and shown on Thanksgiving Day; the Westminster Kennel Club Dog Show is a different event with its own club and its own date.
