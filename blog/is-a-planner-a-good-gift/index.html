Is a Planner a Good Gift? When It Is, and How to Choose One

Is a planner a good gift? Sometimes. Find out who it suits, when to choose one, and what to write inside or on a separate card.

Inkpot Lane · 12 November 2026 · 9 min read

A planner is an unusual sort of present: most of what matters about it has yet to be written. A scarf arrives finished. A novel arrives written. A planner arrives with spaces to fill and a quiet suggestion about next year, and that is why it is easy to hesitate over one in December. Will it look thoughtful, or will it look like a hint? Is a planner a good gift, or is it homework with a ribbon on it? It can be either. The difference lies in three things you can settle before you buy: who it is for, which kind you choose, and whether to add a personal note.

The short answer: Yes, a planner can be a good gift for someone who likes to plan on paper and has talked about something they want to begin: a new year, a job, a course, a business or a retirement. It is a poor gift for someone who runs happily on a phone calendar, or who already loves one particular planner. If you are not sure when they will start, choose an undated one. Pick the size for where they will use it. If they would welcome a note inside, write one before you wrap it; otherwise, put the same words on a separate card.

Is a planner a good gift? When the answer is yes

A planner suits a person at a beginning. Think of the times when someone you know has a clear stretch ahead and something they want to do with it:

  • a new year they have already been talking about;
  • a first job, a new job or a promotion;
  • a course, a degree or an exam year;
  • a business or a side project that is just getting started;
  • a retirement, and the new routines that may come with it.

Here is a test that takes ten seconds. Can you finish the sentence "Next year, they want to..." in their own words? If you can, because they have said it to you, a planner fits. If you are guessing, or if the sentence is really yours ("next year, they should..."), choose something else.

It helps, too, if they already write by hand. Someone who keeps lists on paper or carries a notebook will know what to do with a planner on the first day.

When it is not

Be honest with yourself about four cases.

They run on a phone, and it works. A person whose appointments, reminders and shopping lists all live happily on a phone has no gap for a paper planner to fill.

They already love one kind of planner. Someone who buys the same planner every autumn has already settled every question about layout and size. A different one, however lovely, is likely to sit unused. Ask what they use, and give them next year's.

It could read as a hint. If the unspoken message might be "you should be more organized," the present will sting. If you have ever teased them about being late, a planner is the wrong parcel.

You would be choosing it for yourself. The planner you would love, with a habit grid and a page for the budget, may be exactly the one they would never open. Choose for the way they live.

How to choose a planner for someone else

You can narrow the choice by looking at three things you may already know about them.

Dated or undated. You may not know when they will start. A dated planner given at Christmas suits someone who is ready to begin when its first printed week starts. If it stays in a drawer until March, several weeks of printed dates will already have passed. An undated planner has no first week until its owner writes one in, so it cannot fall behind. When in doubt, give undated. Our guide to the dated vs undated planner sets out both sides.

Size. Think about where they plan. A large planner, the size of a sheet of office paper, gives room to write and belongs on a desk or a kitchen table. A small one travels in a bag. The notebook they already carry is your best clue.

Plain or decorated, a goal or just the dates. Some people want a calendar and nothing else. Some want pages that ask them questions about the year. Match their taste, not yours. If you would like to work through the choice properly, how to choose a planner has five questions that settle it.

Give it started, not blank

One thoughtful touch is to write a little in a gift planner before you wrap it. A new book asks its owner to make the first mark, and the first mark can be the hardest one. A book that already holds a few words in a familiar hand can be easier to begin. If you are not sure they would want their planner written in, put the same words on a separate card.

If you decide to write in the planner, three small things are enough.

  1. A line on the first page, in your own handwriting, saying why you chose it for them.
  2. One date you have already agreed to share. A lunch, a walk, a phone call. Write it on its day in a dated planner, or on the page for important dates in an undated one. (In our own undated planner, that is the set of lines headed "Dates that matter" under the year at a glance.)
  3. Their name, on the line that says who the planner belongs to.

Use a pen, write small, and leave everything else alone. It is their year.

What to write inside a planner you are giving

The message matters more than the wrapping, because it tells them how to read the gift. Here are some lines to borrow or bend. They were written for this page, so take any of them as your own.

For a new year

  • Here is to the year you keep talking about. I cannot wait to watch it happen.
  • For every plan you make this year, and for the ones you change.

For a new job or a new start

  • For the new start you have been talking about, and whatever comes next.
  • You earned this beginning. Fill it your way.

For a student

  • For your classes, your deadlines and the time you want to keep for yourself.

For a friend with a goal

  • You told me about this goal. I remembered.

For a parent, or someone who is retiring

  • For the plans you choose, and the days that unfold differently.

For anyone

  • No rules come with this. Lists, plans or doodles: it is yours.

Keep it to a line or two. The first page should feel like a welcome, not a letter to answer.

When to give it

A dated planner has a first printed week, and some 2027 editions begin before New Year's Day. 1 January 2027 is a Friday, so a dated planner whose weeks start on a Monday may begin on Monday 28 December 2026, three days after Christmas. Given on Christmas morning, it can be set up in the days that follow and opened on its first Monday. It is still perfectly usable if it is opened later; its first printed weeks will simply have passed.

An undated planner has no fixed first week, which is why it also suits a birthday, a graduation or the first day of a new job in any month of the year.

Frequently asked questions

Is a planner a good gift?

It can be, for someone who likes to plan on paper and has told you what they hope to do: a new year with plans in it, a new job, a course, a retirement. It is a poor gift for someone who is content with a phone calendar, or who already has a planner they love.

What does it mean when someone gives you a planner?

A planner does not carry one message for everyone. It may feel like encouragement to someone who has been talking about their plans, and it could feel like a hint to someone who has been criticized for being disorganized. A short personal note makes your intention clear.

What do you write in a planner as a gift?

If they would welcome writing inside the planner, add one or two handwritten lines explaining why you chose it. Otherwise, use a separate card. "Here is to the year you keep talking about" is enough. You can also mark a date you have already agreed to share.

Is a 2027 planner a good Christmas gift?

It is well timed. Because 1 January 2027 is a Friday, a Monday-start 2027 planner may begin on Monday 28 December 2026, three days after Christmas, so there is time to set it up before the first week.

Should I give a dated or an undated planner?

Give a dated planner to someone you know will start with the new year. Give an undated one if you are not sure when they will begin, because it has no first week until they write one in.

What can I give with a planner?

A pen they will enjoy using is the natural partner. Sticky flags or a few colored pens suit someone who likes to decorate a page. Keep the extras small, so that the planner stays the present.
