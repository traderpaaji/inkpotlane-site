How to Choose a Planner for 2027: Five Questions to Ask

How to choose a planner for 2027: five questions about your schedule, your start date, the size, your goals and the page itself, to ask before you buy.

Inkpot Lane · 12 November 2026 · 10 min read

Picture a planner that has been in a drawer since March. The first weeks are full. Then comes a page with two lines on it, then a blank one, then nine months of clean paper. It is easy to read that drawer as a verdict on yourself. It may only mean the planner was the wrong shape for the weeks you actually live. Two 2027 planners can look alike from the front and work very differently inside, so it is worth learning how to choose a planner before you look at a single cover. It comes down to five plain questions.

The short answer: Choose a planner by how you plan, not by how it looks. Ask five things. Days: is your day run by the clock or by a list? Dates: will you start in January, and what happens when you miss a week? Place: will it live on a desk or travel in a bag? Purpose: is next year about keeping appointments or about reaching a goal? Page: how much do you want printed for you, and how much left blank? Your answers name the layout, the size and the kind of planner before any brand does.

How to choose a planner: start with the one you stopped using

Before the five questions, find last year's planner, or the one from the year before, and turn to the last page you wrote on. It will often show one of three things.

  • You ran out of room. Notes climb the margins and arrows run on to the next day. The boxes were too small for your days.
  • You ran out of things to put in it. Whole sections were never touched: a tracker, a budget page, a row of prompts. The planner asked for more than you wanted to give it.
  • It was never where you were. The pages are fine, and almost empty. It lived on a shelf while you planned on the sofa, or it stayed at home while your day happened somewhere else.

Each may tell you something about the fit, though a planner can also go unused for reasons that have nothing to do with its design. The questions below help you check. If the trouble was a run of blank dated pages after one busy month, that is question 2.

Question 1: is your day run by the clock or by a list?

This is the question behind the choice between a daily and a weekly planner.

A daily planner gives each day a space of its own, usually a page. Some print the hours down the side; others leave the page open for lists and notes. The hourly kind suits a day made of appointments: clients, classes, shifts, calls. The trade-off is bulk. A page a day is 365 pages before anything else is added.

A weekly planner shows seven days together, sometimes on one page and sometimes across two facing pages, with a few lines for each. It suits a day made of tasks: three or four things that matter, done in whatever order the day allows. You see the whole week at once, which makes it easy to move Tuesday's job to Thursday.

A monthly planner is a calendar grid and little else. It holds dates, not days, and suits someone who only needs to see what is coming.

A useful test: think back over yesterday. Did you need to see where appointments fitted between particular hours, or did you mostly need a list of things to finish? If the timing mattered, look for a planner with the hours printed. If the order was flexible, look at a weekly layout.

Question 2: will you start in January, and what happens when you miss a week?

A dated planner has the days printed in. An undated planner has the same kind of pages with the dates left blank, and you write them in as you go. The dated one is ready the moment you open it. The undated one forgives a late start and a missed week, because a week you skip leaves no blank dated page behind. We have set out the honest pros and cons of each in dated vs undated planner.

For 2027 there is one more thing to check. 1 January 2027 falls on a Friday. A dated planner whose weeks run Monday to Sunday may begin on Monday 28 December 2026, open with a part week, or start its full weeks on Monday 4 January 2027. Look at its first printed page to see how it handles the days around New Year's Day.

Question 3: will it live on a desk or travel in a bag?

Size decides two things at once: how much you can write, and whether the planner is with you when you need it. Two sizes are worth knowing.

  • Letter size, 8.5 x 11 inches. The size of a sheet of office paper in the United States. The lines are long and the boxes are roomy. It may be awkward to carry in a smaller bag.
  • A5, 148 x 210 millimeters, which is about 5.8 x 8.3 inches. It is half an A4 sheet. It fits in a bag, and what you give up is room: a whole week across two pages gets tight.

Binding matters for the same reason. A spiral or wire binding lets a book fold back on itself and lie flat. A glued paperback spine opens like any paperback, so it may need a hand or a clip to hold it flat on the desk.

So ask where you plan. If the answer is "at the kitchen table on Sunday evening," a large planner that never leaves the house is the right one. If the answer is "wherever I am when someone asks if I am free on the 14th," it has to fit the bag, and everything else comes second.

Question 4: is next year about appointments or about a goal?

Planners divide here more sharply than their covers suggest.

  • A calendar planner holds dates and lists. If your aim is to stop missing things, this is all you need, and extra pages only get in the way.
  • A goal planner adds pages before the calendar starts: somewhere to choose a goal, break it into steps, and look back on it each month or quarter. Then the weekly pages ask what you did about it. In our own 2027 goal planner, for instance, every weekly spread has a place for three priorities carried down from that quarter's goals, beside the days themselves.
  • A goal workbook has no calendar at all. It takes one goal apart in detail over a few weeks, and you use it beside whatever calendar you already keep.

One honest warning. Goal pages earn their place only if you fill them in. If you know you will skip them, choose the calendar planner and feel no guilt about it. And if you are not sure what the goal is yet, how to set goals for the new year is a better first step than any purchase.

Question 5: how much do you want printed on the page?

Open two planners side by side and the plainest difference is how much each page already says. Some print prompts, trackers and a line of encouragement on every spread. Some print lines and nothing else.

Printed structure helps if you like to be asked. A habit grid you fill in each evening is a quiet push. The same grid left empty for a month becomes a daily reminder of a plan you dropped. So look at one ordinary week in the planner and ask of each box on it: would I fill this in on a tired Wednesday?

Decoration is the same question in a different form. Color illustrations or plain black and white is a matter of taste. If you like to use highlighters, colored pens or stickers of your own, a plain page gives them somewhere to show.

The best planner for 2027 is a type, not a brand

There is no single best planner for 2027. There is only the one that fits your five answers. Put them together like this.

When you know the type, check three things on the real pages before you buy. Look at the first dated page: where does the year begin? Look at one full weekly or daily spread: is there room for your handwriting? And look at the pages before the calendar starts: will you use them?

Frequently asked questions

How do I choose a planner?

Start from how you plan, not from the cover. Decide whether your days run by the clock or by a list, whether you will start in January, where the planner will live, whether the year is about appointments or a goal, and how much you want printed on the page. Those five answers narrow the choice to one type.

Is a daily or a weekly planner better?

Neither is better in general. If your day is full of fixed appointments, look for a daily planner with hourly spaces. If you plan mainly by tasks, a weekly layout lets you see and rearrange seven days together, whether on one page or two.

What size planner should I get?

Choose the size for the place you plan. Letter size, 8.5 x 11 inches, gives the most room and suits a desk. A5, about 5.8 x 8.3 inches, fits a bag and has less room for a full week.

What is the best planner for 2027?

The one that matches the way you plan. Someone with a day of appointments needs the hours printed. Someone working toward one goal needs goal pages and a weekly review. Someone who starts late or skips weeks is better served by an undated planner.

When should I buy a 2027 planner?

Buy one when you are ready to begin using it. If you want to start with the first printed week of a dated 2027 planner, check whether that week begins in December 2026: 1 January 2027 is a Friday, so a Monday-start planner may begin on Monday 28 December.

Is a paper planner better than a digital one?

Not in general. A phone calendar is good at reminders and at events you share with other people. A paper planner shows a whole week at once and is written by hand. Some people keep both: appointments on the phone, priorities on paper.
