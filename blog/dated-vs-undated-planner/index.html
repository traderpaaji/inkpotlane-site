Dated vs Undated Planner: Which One Will You Actually Use?

Dated vs undated planner: what a missed week looks like in each, the honest pros and cons, and how to choose the one you will actually keep using.

Inkpot Lane · 10 September 2026 · 4 min read

You miss ten days. Nothing dramatic happened: work ran late, somebody got sick, the weekend disappeared, and the planner stayed closed. Now you open it again, and a dated vs undated planner will treat those ten days very differently.

In a dated planner, those days are still there waiting for you: printed dates, empty boxes, visible evidence of the gap. Some people draw a line through them and turn the page without a second thought. For others, that row of blank dates is exactly where the planner starts feeling like something they failed.

In an undated planner, the next blank spread is simply the next week. You write today's date and carry on.

That small difference — not productivity philosophy, not willpower — is what you are really deciding between.

The short answer: a dated planner has the days printed in, and suits people who start in January and plan most weeks. An undated planner leaves the dates blank for you to fill in, and suits people who start later or tend to skip weeks, because a missed week leaves no empty pages behind.

Dated vs undated planner: what you are really choosing

The difference is simple.

A dated planner has the days and weeks printed for you. You open it and go.

An undated planner has the same kind of pages with the dates left blank. You write in the Monday you begin, and every week follows on from there.

The layout, the goal pages and the reviews can be identical. Only the dates change, and with them, what a missed week looks like.

The honest downside of each

Dated planners leave a scar. A slow patch shows up as blank, dated pages that you have to look past every time you open the book. Some people shrug that off. Others never open the book again.

Undated planners ask for one small job. You have to write the date each week. For some people that is a pleasant little ritual. For others it's exactly the kind of friction that keeps a book shut. And with nothing printed, it's easier to lose track of which month you're in, unless the planner has month pages to anchor you.

How to choose: ask the April question

A dated planner probably fits you if:

  • you want to start in January, and you usually do;
  • you like a book that's ready with no setup;
  • your year runs on fixed dates such as school terms, bills or appointments.

An undated planner probably fits you if:

  • you're buying after January has already begun;
  • you have a history of skipped weeks;
  • your schedule shifts with night shifts, travel, a new baby or exams;
  • you would rather start on the day you feel ready than on the day the calendar says.

If you're still unsure, ask one question: what did my last planner look like in April? The answer usually decides it.

Frequently asked questions

What is an undated planner?

An undated planner is a planner with no days or dates printed on its pages. Instead of a fixed start date, you write in the date of the Monday (or day) you begin, and each week simply follows on from there. That means you can start in March, June or the day after your birthday, and a week you miss leaves no blank, dated page behind.

What is the difference between a dated and an undated planner?

A dated planner has the days and weeks printed in. An undated planner has blank dates, so you write in the date of the week you start and carry on from there.

Is an undated planner better?

It depends on how you plan. An undated planner is better if you start mid-year or tend to skip weeks, because missed weeks leave no blank pages. A dated planner is better if you start in January and want the book ready without writing any dates.

Why does a 2027 planner have 53 weeks?

1 January 2027 falls on a Friday, and so does 31 December. To cover every day of the year in Monday-to-Sunday weeks, the first week has to start on Monday 28 December 2026 and the last has to end on Sunday 2 January 2028. That makes 53 weeks.

Can I use an undated planner for 2027?

Yes. An undated planner works for any year. Write in the Monday you start and continue week by week.
